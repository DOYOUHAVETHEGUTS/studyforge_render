Chapter 8 — TCP/IP Applications

Network+ Guide · pp. 231–268 · 5-page summary
✓ Grounded to source · 0 unsupported claims

Chapter Overview

This chapter covers the transport and application layers of TCP/IP, focusing on how ports and protocols map real-world services to the OSI model. It aligns to the N10-009 objectives on ports, protocols, and traffic types — a high-yield exam area.

Ports and Protocols You Must Memorize

The exam expects rapid recall of common ports. Group them by function rather than memorizing a flat list: file transfer (FTP 20/21, SFTP 22, TFTP 69), remote access (SSH 22, Telnet 23, RDP 3389), mail (SMTP 25, POP3 110, IMAP4 143), and web (HTTP 80, HTTPS 443).

  • Telnet vs SSH: both give command-line remote access, but Telnet is cleartext; SSH encrypts the whole session and has effectively replaced it.
  • POP3 vs IMAP4: POP3 downloads and typically removes mail; IMAP4 synchronizes across devices — the modern standard.
  • Active vs passive FTP: passive FTP is NAT/firewall-friendly because the client always initiates the data connection.

Connection States & Troubleshooting

Use netstat -ano to inspect active connections. Recognize LISTENING (ready), ESTABLISHED (active), and FIN-based closing states. Modern Linux uses ss; TCPView is the Windows GUI alternative.
